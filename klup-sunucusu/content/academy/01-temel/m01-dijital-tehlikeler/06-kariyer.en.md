# What jobs are there in cyber security?

![A cutaway view of an office building; a different security role is at work in each room](../../gorseller/m01-06-b.webp)

> Cyber security isn't one profession — it's **a city made of many professions**.
> Even if you don't love writing code, there may be a corner of it that suits you.

::cizgi[m01-06-sahne]

| Role | What's a day like? | Track in the academy |
|---|---|---|
| **SOC analyst** | Reviews alerts and picks out the real threats | C |
| **Penetration tester** (pentester) | Attacks systems with permission and writes reports | A, B |
| **Incident responder** | Works out what's happening during an attack and stops it | C |
| **Digital forensics specialist** | Examines digital evidence | C |
| **Threat intelligence analyst** | Tracks attacker groups and their methods | D |
| **Application security engineer** | Helps developers write secure code | A |
| **Cloud / AI security** | Manages the risks of new technologies | E |
| **Reverse engineer** | Takes malware apart to see how it works | G |
| **Security consultant / GRC** | Policy, risk and compliance | All tracks + M02 |

## What counts when you're hired?

- Work you **can show**: reports, CTF solutions, small projects → that's exactly what your capstone project is.
- Fundamentals: operating systems, networks, the web. → That's what the core part is for.
- Communication: being able to explain what you found to a non-technical person.

Certificates help, but they **don't replace a portfolio**.

## 🧪 Try it (3 min)

Pick the **two** roles from the table that appeal to you most. For each, write one sentence answering "why?".
Come back to this note when you choose a track in week 15.

## ✅ Remember

- Defence, offence, research, management: there's a role for every inclination.
- Work you can show > certificates alone.
- Fundamentals are the shared language of every role.
