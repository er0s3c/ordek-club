# How does your digital footprint give you away?

> Every photo, like and location you share leaves a **trace**.
> Traces that look harmless on their own tell a story when you put them side by side.

::cizgi[m01-03-sahne]

## Three concepts

![A student silhouette made of profile cards, glowing footprints behind it, and a crowd examining the footprints with magnifying glasses](../../gorseller/m01-03-b.webp)

- **Digital identity** — Everything known as "you" online: your accounts, usernames, email address.
- **Digital footprint** — The traces you leave.
  - *Active:* what you share yourself.
  - *Passive:* what you leave without noticing — location history, cookies, app permissions.
- **Online reputation** — The conclusions others draw about you from these traces (e.g. recruiters).

## What can a scammer learn?

![Three photos joining like puzzle pieces to form a map leading to a dorm building](../../gorseller/m01-03-c.webp)

Think about these three posts:

1. A photo of your new campus card 📸
2. A story saying "Waiting for my scholarship results 🤞"
3. The view from your dorm room window

Put together: **which school you're at, that you're expecting a scholarship, and where you live.**
That's enough for a fake scholarship message tailored just for you (see the next pages).

## Ways to shrink your footprint

- Review your accounts' privacy settings; take your profile off "public".
- **Don't share** photos of IDs, cards, tickets or ticket barcodes.
- Share your location **afterwards**, not live.
- Remove permissions from apps you don't use and close those accounts.

## 🧪 Try it (5 min)

Type **your full name in quotes** into a search engine and look at the first two pages.
What surprised you? Is there anything you'd like removed?

## ✅ Remember

- Every post is a trace; traces put together tell a story.
- Passive traces (location, permissions) are the least noticed.
- Share less, share later, share privately.
