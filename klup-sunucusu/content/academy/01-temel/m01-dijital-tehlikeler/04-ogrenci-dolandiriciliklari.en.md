# Scams that target students

> Scammers follow the calendar closely: enrolment, scholarship results, the hunt for a room, internship season.
> The message arrives **exactly when you're expecting it**.

::cizgi[m01-04-sahne]

## The most common ones

| Type | How does it arrive? | What does it want? |
|---|---|---|
| 🎓 **Fake scholarship** | SMS / email: "You've won a scholarship, confirm it" | Your school account password, card details |
| 🏠 **Fake rental** | A cheap listing with lovely photos | Send the "deposit" right now |
| 💼 **Fake job / internship** | "Work from home, €50 a day" | A photo of your ID, an upfront payment |
| 🛍️ **Fake sale** | A cheap phone or ticket | Money before shipping |
| 📦 **Parcel / invoice** | "Your parcel is waiting, pay €1.99" | Card details |
| 🔑 **Account warning** | "Your account will be closed!" | Password and verification code |

## The signs they all share

![A suspicious envelope covered in five warning symbols: an hourglass, an oversized bag of gold, a tangled chain link, a keyhole asking for a key and an odd carrier pigeon](../../gorseller/m01-04-b.webp)

1. **Urgency** — "within 24 hours", "last chance".
2. **Too good to be true** — far below market price, easy money.
3. **An unknown link** — an odd or shortened domain.
4. **A request for secret information** — password, verification code, card number.
5. **An unusual channel** — like your school, which never asks for passwords by SMS.

## 🧪 Try it (3 min)

Think of a message you received in the last month (your spam folder counts).
How many of the 5 signs above did it have?

## ✅ Remember

- If a message arrives "right on time", be extra careful.
- Urgency + a request for secret information = alarm.
- When in doubt, don't click the link; go to the organisation's official site **yourself**.
