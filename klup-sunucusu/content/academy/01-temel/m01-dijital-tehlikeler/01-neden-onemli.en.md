# Why does cyber security matter to you?

> A hacker's target usually isn't "someone important" — it's **anyone with an open door**.
> And all of us carry a few doors in our pockets.

## What does cyber security mean?

Protecting your information, accounts and devices against **unauthorised access, tampering and being made unusable**.

Picture a morning:
- Your phone signs in to your student email automatically.
- Your laptop holds your assignments, your photos and your banking app.
- Your Instagram password is probably the same on five other sites.

If **one** of these is taken over, the chain can reach the others.

## Where do threats come from?

![The three parts of an incident: a chest full of valuables, its broken hinge, and an opportunist's hand reaching out of the shadows](../../gorseller/m01-01-b.webp)

Almost every incident is three parts meeting:

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| **Something valuable** | Your account, your data, your scholarship payment |
| **A weakness** | A reused password, a phone that hasn't been updated |
| **Someone looking for an opening** | A scammer sending fake messages in bulk |

Attackers mostly don't try people **one by one** — they try **thousands of people at once**.
All they need is to find the one with the open door. Your job: not to be that person.

## The good news

Most incidents can be prevented with a few simple habits:
strong, different passwords, two-step verification, an up-to-date device and a **"wait, let me check"** reflex.

## 🧪 Try it (3 min)

Count your accounts: email, social media, the school system, games, shopping…
On how many of them do you use **the same password**? Write the number down; in M08 we'll bring it down to zero.

## ✅ Remember

- Cyber security is everyone's business; you don't have to be famous to be a target.
- Incident = something valuable + a weakness + an opportunist.
- Simple habits stop most incidents.
