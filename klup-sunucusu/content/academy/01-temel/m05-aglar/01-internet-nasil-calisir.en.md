# What is the internet, really?

> The internet isn't one thing; it's the shared name for **millions of networks joined together**.
> Your dorm room is one of them.

## Small network, big network

- **LAN** (local area network) — Your home, your dorm, the lab. Devices see each other directly.
- **WAN** (wide area network) — Links between cities and countries. The internet is the biggest WAN.

## The devices and their jobs

![Network devices as a city: a modem at the city gate, a router as a traffic officer at the crossroads, a switch as the building's letterboxes, and an access point as the radio mast on the roof](../../gorseller/m05-01-b.webp)

| Device | Analogy | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Modem** | The gate opening onto the city | Turns the internet provider's line into a signal the devices understand |
| **Router** | The officer at the crossroads | Directs packets to the right network; sits between your home and the internet |
| **Switch** | The building's letterboxes | Delivers to the right device among devices on the same network |
| **Access point** | A radio transmitter | Connects wireless devices to the network |

The box at home that everyone calls "the modem" often does **all of these at once**.

## Ethernet or Wi-Fi?

| | Ethernet (cable) | Wi-Fi (wireless) |
|---|---|---|
| Speed and reliability | Higher | Drops with distance and walls |
| Security | Needs physical access to the cable | The signal reaches beyond the wall |

That's why you need to be careful on shared Wi-Fi (a café, a dorm) — M08 and Path B go into detail.

## 🧪 Try it (3 min)

Look at the network device in your home or dorm: which lights are on it, how many Ethernet ports does it have?
Which jobs (modem, router, switch, AP) might it be doing?

## ✅ Remember

- Internet = a network of interconnected networks.
- A router routes between networks; a switch delivers within a network.
- A Wi-Fi signal reaches beyond the wall; be careful on shared networks.
