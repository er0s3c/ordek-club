# Lab rules

> The lab is where you can **break things safely**. That safety is protected by the rules below.

![An isolated lab inside a glass dome; the Shadow Network Family pushes against the glass from outside but cannot get in](../gorseller/p-04-a.webp)

## The golden rule

**No permission, no testing.** You work only on:

- Your own computer and your own virtual machines
- The club's isolated lab and Ördek Lab targets
- Systems whose scope is clear and for which you have written permission

The university's real systems, the campus Wi-Fi and other students' accounts are **out of scope** —
including "I was only looking".

## In the lab

- Vulnerable applications are not connected to real systems and are not exposed to the internet.
- Real passwords, real personal data and traffic collected without permission are **never** used — everything is synthetic.
- If you see something that goes beyond the target you were given, stop and tell your instructor.
- Reset your environment when you're done; leave it clean for whoever comes next.

## If you find a flaw

If you accidentally notice a problem on a real system: **don't try it, don't spread it, don't try to collect evidence.**
Report it to the club organisers; we'll go through the responsible disclosure process together (M02).

## Why so serious?

The same command, done with permission, is a **security test**; done without it, it's a **crime**.
The difference isn't in the technique, it's in the authorisation.
